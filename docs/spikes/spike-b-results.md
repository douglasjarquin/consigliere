This spike proves `Consigliere.MissionCoordinator`'s crash (and subsequent supervisor restart) never terminates its Mission's `Consigliere.RunnerProcess` or that process's real OS-level harness subprocess, and that the restarted coordinator safely re-attaches to the same still-running runner, per `docs/phase0-report.md` section 11's Spike B design and ADR-004.

## What was built

- `daemon/lib/consigliere/runner_process.ex`: a GenServer wrapping an Erlang Port that spawns a fake harness subprocess (`daemon/priv/fake_harness.sh`), registers itself in `Consigliere.Registry` under `{:runner, attempt_id}`. Overrides `child_spec/1` to set `restart: :temporary` (an Attempt is disposable per ADR-004; its death must never trigger an implicit same-identity respawn). Distinguishes a clean exit (`exit_status: 0`, stops `:normal`) from a crash (`exit_status: nonzero`, stops `{:harness_exited, status}`).
- `daemon/lib/consigliere/runner_dynamic_supervisor.ex` and `mission_dynamic_supervisor.ex`: two independent, top-level `DynamicSupervisor`s (`:one_for_one`), true siblings under `Consigliere.Supervisor`, confirmed live via `Supervisor.which_children/1`.
- `daemon/lib/consigliere/mission_coordinator.ex`: a GenServer representing one Mission. On `init/1`, looks up its Attempt's `RunnerProcess` via `Registry.lookup/2` (never starts, links, or supervises it) and caches the result (a pid, or `:not_found`).
- `daemon/lib/consigliere/mission_supervisor.ex` (added in round 2, see finding B1 below): a plain, non-dynamic `Supervisor` wrapping exactly one `MissionCoordinator`, with its own scoped restart intensity, `restart: :temporary` (added in round 3) and `type: :supervisor`/`shutdown: :infinity` (added in round 4, see below) at the `MissionDynamicSupervisor` level. `MissionCoordinator` itself stays `restart: :permanent` (unlike `RunnerProcess`), since criterion 2 depends on it being auto-restarted and reattaching.
- `Consigliere.MissionDynamicSupervisor.start_mission/1` (added in round 3): the one sanctioned way to start a mission subtree, so no future caller can bypass `MissionSupervisor`'s isolation by starting `MissionCoordinator` directly under `MissionDynamicSupervisor` — this exact regression happened twice before this function existed. Every call site in this diff goes through it; see `daemon/test/consigliere/mission_dynamic_supervisor_test.exs` (added in round 3 alongside `start_mission/1`; gained a second test in round 4 for the `type: :supervisor` fix below).
- `Consigliere.Registry` added to `Consigliere.Application`'s `:one_for_one` children, alongside the existing `Repo`/`DatabaseWriter` from Spike A.

## Verification-gate round 1: rejection, and what it caught

A strict adversarial review (`omo:reviewer`, substituting for the unavailable `codex-ultrawork-reviewer`) rejected the first submission with reproduced, not hypothetical, findings:

1. **Blast-radius bug (fixed)**: `RunnerDynamicSupervisor`'s restart intensity (default `max_restarts: 3, max_seconds: 5`) was supervisor-wide, and `RunnerProcess` was `restart: :permanent` (the `use GenServer` default), so repeatedly crashing one runner's OS harness eventually exhausted the shared intensity budget and killed every other runner under the same supervisor, the exact blast-radius class this spike exists to prevent (Symphony's `:one_for_all` failure mode, arriving via restart intensity instead of strategy). **Fixed** by making `RunnerProcess` `restart: :temporary`: a dead Attempt is never implicitly respawned under the same identity, so there is no restart-intensity budget to exhaust for this child type at all. Proven with a new adversarial test, `daemon/test/consigliere/runner_isolation_test.exs`, which went RED against the old code (an innocent runner's BEAM pid died as collateral damage) and GREEN after the fix.
2. **Silent harness-exit resurrection (fixed)**: because `RunnerProcess` was `:permanent` and treated every harness exit (including a clean `exit_status: 0`) as a crash, a harness finishing its work would cause the supervisor to silently spawn a brand-new OS harness under the same `attempt_id`/Registry key, with no fencing to detect the swap. This was the direct fuel for finding 1. **Fixed** by the same `restart: :temporary` change, plus distinguishing `exit_status: 0` (stops `:normal`) from a nonzero status (stops `{:harness_exited, status}`).
3. **Registry-kill brings down the whole app (investigated, accepted as intentional fail-closed behavior)**: killing `Consigliere.Registry`'s own top pid does not instantly kill its internal `Registry.PIDPartition0` child; that process does receive the propagated `:killed` exit and does die, but slowly (blocked loading `code_server`/`sys.beam`/`Logger.Translator` at the moment of the kill), so the supervisor's restart attempt collides with `{:already_started, <the still-dying pid>}` three times before it finishes exiting, exhausting `Consigliere.Supervisor`'s own restart intensity and shutting down the entire application. Traced precisely via `:erlang.trace` on the supervisor's receive loop, independently re-confirmed by the round-2 verification-gate reviewer (see notepad for both traces). This is an artifact of an adversarial, untrappable `Process.exit(pid, :kill)` against Registry's own supervisor pid specifically, racing against how slowly its child happens to die; a real Registry crash goes through normal `:shutdown`/`terminate` handling and is very unlikely to hit this exact timing window. Per this project's own design principle ("daemon restart is routine and safe," master-prompt Phase 1/2 exit gates), a clean full-application shutdown here is the correct fail-closed outcome, not a hidden failure mode: Phase 1/2's reconciler is exactly what is supposed to handle recovery after a daemon restart. **Not patched further in Spike B**, and no automated regression test was added for this specific case (an earlier notepad entry proposed one; that plan item is explicitly retracted here, not silently dropped, because exercising it safely would require killing the whole test application mid-suite, which is disproportionate machinery for a Phase 0 spike to add just to pin an accepted, already-traced-twice limitation); flagged here explicitly as an investigated, understood, and accepted Phase 0 limitation, with the precise root cause on record so nobody has to re-derive it later.
4. **Weak topology test (fixed)**: criterion 1's original test only asserted "coordinator killed, runner still alive," which would not have caught `RunnerProcess` being wrongly nested under `MissionDynamicSupervisor` (a `:one_for_one` sibling child would also survive a coordinator kill). **Fixed** by adding structural assertions (child-of-`RunnerDynamicSupervisor`, not-child-of-`MissionDynamicSupervisor`, no OTP link to the coordinator) and verifying, by deliberately sabotaging the topology and reverting, that the new assertions actually catch the regression.
5. **Cached-pid liveness assertion (fixed)**: criterion 2's OS-pid check compared a cached integer in `RunnerProcess` state, which would pass even against a dead process. **Fixed** by adding a real `kill -0` check plus an advancing-heartbeat-count check after reattachment.
6. **Missing monitor, ordering assumption (documented, deferred)**: `MissionCoordinator` caches `runner_pid` once at `init` with no `Process.monitor/1`, though `docs/architecture/runtime.md` specifies the coordinator should monitor the runner to react promptly to its exit. The current design also assumes the runner is already registered before the coordinator starts; in real scheduling order this won't always hold, and there is no attach-later path if the runner isn't there yet. **Both are explicit, named, deferred-to-Phase-1/2 gaps**, not silently accepted: adding a real monitor and an attach-later mechanism is meaningful additional design work (deciding what the coordinator does on `{:DOWN, ...}`, and how/when it retries an attach) that belongs with the rest of Phase 1's Mission/Attempt lifecycle logic, not bolted onto this narrowly-scoped spike.
7. **Evidence-integrity defects (fixed)**: the notepad falsely claimed criterion 3 was exercised via the tmux driver (it was not; retracted). The driver script's own `IO.puts` had an inverted boolean label (printed "same as before? true" while the code meant "different"), so the raw transcript self-contradicted without the notepad's parenthetical gloss. Fixed the label and re-captured a clean, self-consistent transcript (`...-round2.txt`).
8. **Formatting (fixed)**: `mix format` now passes clean (`mix format --check-formatted` exits 0) across the whole `daemon/` project, including files pre-existing on `rewrite-in-elixer` that were not part of this diff, per this project's engineering-excellence standard of fixing lint issues encountered along the way.
9. **Cleanup discipline (fixed)**: added a shared `test/support/process_helpers.ex` (`kill_and_verify_dead/1`, `wait_until_dead/2`) used by every test file and the tmux driver, so every OS-level kill is followed by a polled verification, not a single best-effort attempt. Added `DynamicSupervisor.terminate_child/2` calls for both `RunnerProcess` and `MissionCoordinator` in every test's `on_exit`. Added the previously-missing `on_exit` to `mission_coordinator_test.exs`. Fixed `runner_process_test.exs`'s hardcoded `attempt_id` to be unique per run.

## Verification-gate round 2: rejection (one serious bug, several secondary items)

**B1 (serious, partially fixed this round; fully fixed in round 3, see below)**: the round-1 blast-radius fix only covered `RunnerProcess`. `MissionCoordinator` was still `restart: :permanent` under `MissionDynamicSupervisor`'s default shared `max_restarts: 3, max_seconds: 5`, so a flapping coordinator could kill every unrelated mission's coordinator, and could exhaust the top-level supervisor's own budget too, taking the whole application down. The reviewer reproduced this with an A/B root-cause confirmation (bumping `max_restarts` on a copy flipped a reliably-failing scenario to 15/15 green) and explicitly flagged that `restart: :temporary` is **not** the fix for `MissionCoordinator` itself, since criterion 2 depends on the coordinator being auto-restarted. This round's fix added `Consigliere.MissionSupervisor` (see "What was built" above): a per-mission `Supervisor` subtree isolates restart-intensity accounting per mission without removing coordinator auto-restart. Proven with a new adversarial test, `daemon/test/consigliere/mission_isolation_test.exs`, mirroring `runner_isolation_test.exs`: RED against the old code (innocent mission's coordinator died as collateral damage after 4 rapid kills of an unrelated mission's coordinator), GREEN after the fix. **This fix turned out to be incomplete** — see the round-3 section below for what it missed and how it was actually closed.

- **S4**: `spike_b_coordinator_independence_test.exs`'s `on_exit` called `terminate_child` then waited for death with no explicit kill first, relying on the incidental SIGPIPE death this project's own findings warn against. Made consistent with `kill_and_verify_dead/1` like every other file.

## Verification-gate round 3: rejection (B1 was deferred, not eliminated)

**B1, take two (fixed for real this time)**: round 2's `MissionSupervisor` fix scoped restart-intensity accounting *within* each mission's own subtree, but `MissionSupervisor` itself was still `restart: :permanent` (the `use Supervisor` default) under `MissionDynamicSupervisor`'s own shared `max_restarts: 3, max_seconds: 5`. A sustained crash loop past the *inner* threshold (the reviewer reproduced with 16 kills at ~100ms, deeper than round 2's 4-kill test) makes one mission's `MissionSupervisor` give up; `MissionDynamicSupervisor` then restarts *that*, and after three such give-ups inside 5 seconds `MissionDynamicSupervisor` itself exceeds its own budget and is torn down, taking every mission with it — the identical blast radius, one supervision level higher. The reviewer proved this two independent ways (the project's own `mission_isolation_test.exs` failing on iteration 4 of `--repeat-until-failure 10`, and their own 16-kill probe) and did their own A/B: adding `restart: :temporary` to `MissionSupervisor.child_spec/1` on a scratch copy fixed it completely. **Fixed** by exactly that: `MissionSupervisor` now overrides `child_spec/1` to `restart: :temporary`, mirroring `RunnerProcess`'s own reasoning one level up — a subtree that has genuinely given up must never be blindly resurrected by a supervisor with a budget shared across unrelated subtrees. Criterion 2 is unaffected: the coordinator's own auto-restart still happens *inside* `MissionSupervisor`, below the threshold where it would give up. `mission_isolation_test.exs`'s loop was strengthened from 4 kills at 200ms to 16 kills at 100ms (the shallower version was non-vacuous for round 2's topology but didn't pin the deeper invariant); RED against the round-3 code, GREEN after this fix.

**B2, take two**: the round-2 "100/100 same-VM" evidence was itself blind to this deeper cascade, since each full-suite iteration only produced one `MissionDynamicSupervisor`-level restart (three are needed inside 5 seconds to trigger it) — the same class of blind evidence rejected in round 2, one level up. **Fixed**: `mix test test/consigliere/mission_isolation_test.exs --repeat-until-failure 20` (same VM, 320 total crash-loop kills across the run) now passes 20/20.

**Cleanup leak (fixed)**: the round-2 evidence run had silently leaked one `MissionSupervisor`/`MissionCoordinator` pair per iteration, since `MissionDynamicSupervisor` was recreating a replacement subtree under the same `mission_id` on every restart that the test's `on_exit` never tracked. This is resolved as a direct consequence of the real fix above: once `MissionSupervisor` is `restart: :temporary`, no replacement is ever created, so the originally-captured supervisor pid remains the one true (and, once given up, permanently dead) subtree for that mission. Verified directly: a 16-kill probe against a fresh mission shows `DynamicSupervisor.count_children/1`'s active count unchanged before and after the crash loop (net zero, not net one-per-iteration).

## Verification-gate round 4: rejection (real fix confirmed correct; no fourth level exists; two mechanical defects)

Explicitly asked to hunt for a third level of the same bug class (beyond the runner-side and mission-side fixes above), the reviewer enumerated every shared-restart-budget supervisor in the tree and tried to exhaust each: duplicate-`mission_id` start churn, 50 start/terminate cycles, and up to 80 kills against one mission's coordinator. **No further level exists** — every child that could ever be resurrected under a shared budget (`RunnerProcess`, `MissionSupervisor`) is now `restart: :temporary`, so the restart-intensity counter can never increment from this workload, structurally, not just empirically. Directly killing `MissionDynamicSupervisor` itself 4 times in 5 seconds does cascade to `Consigliere.Supervisor`, but this is the same accepted adversarial class as the Registry-kill limitation below (an intentional, direct multi-kill of a top-level child), not a new workload-driven path.

Two blocking findings, both mechanical, both fixed:

- **B1**: `MissionSupervisor.child_spec/1`'s override dropped `type: :supervisor`, so it was registered as a `:worker` with a 5-second brutal-kill shutdown instead of the `:infinity` a supervisor child needs to shut its own children down cleanly. **Fixed**: added `type: :supervisor, shutdown: :infinity` to the child spec. New test `daemon/test/consigliere/mission_dynamic_supervisor_test.exs` (`"MissionSupervisor is registered as a :supervisor child, not :worker"`) RED against the round-4 code, GREEN after.
- **B2**: round 3's switch to `MissionDynamicSupervisor.start_mission/1` left unused `alias Consigliere.MissionSupervisor` in three test files. `mix compile --warnings-as-errors`, the lint evidence cited through round 3, never compiles `test/**/*.exs` and was structurally blind to this — the same class of blind evidence rejected in rounds 2 and 3, this time in the lint lane. **Fixed**: removed the dead aliases; `mix test --warnings-as-errors` (which does compile and run every test file) is now the standing lint evidence, confirmed exit 0 with zero warnings.

## Verification-gate round 5: rejection (documentation-only; no code defect found)

Every engineering item from round 4 was independently re-verified from scratch — the `type: :supervisor`/`shutdown: :infinity` fix confirmed live via `:sys.get_state`, the new test confirmed non-vacuous by re-sabotaging it, no unused aliases anywhere, `mix test --warnings-as-errors` clean, and the architectural fix re-confirmed under an even deeper adversarial load than round 4 used (a 25-kill probe with two innocent missions and a live OS-harness runner as bystanders, plus a separate 60-kill combined probe against both `MissionSupervisor` and `RunnerProcess` pids) — zero cascade, zero leaked children, all innocent parties unaffected in every case. **No code defect was found in this round.**

The one blocking finding was in this document itself: the known-limitations wording added in round 4 (below) made a specific, wrong numeric claim. It said any top-level child killed "three or more times" exhausts the shared budget, and described `Consigliere.Registry` as "the clearest instance" of that same rule. Both are wrong: an ordinary child survives exactly 3 kills and only fails on the 4th (`max_restarts: 3` tolerates 3, not "3 or more"), and `Registry` is not a smaller case of the same rule at all — it goes down on a single kill, because the `{:already_started, ...}` collision consumes the entire budget internally from that one external kill. **Fixed**: corrected both numbers and the Registry framing below, and corrected two round-attribution off-by-ones in "What was built" (`mission_supervisor.ex` was added in round 2, not round 3; `start_mission/1` was added in round 3, not round 4) caught in the same pass.

## Known limitations carried forward (not silently accepted)

- `MissionCoordinator` does not yet `Process.monitor/1` its runner and has no attach-later path if the runner isn't registered yet at coordinator init time. Deferred to Phase 1 alongside the rest of Mission/Attempt lifecycle logic.
- Directly killing any ordinary top-level child of `Consigliere.Supervisor` (for example `Consigliere.DatabaseWriter` or `MissionDynamicSupervisor` itself) **four or more** times within 5 seconds exhausts `Consigliere.Supervisor`'s own `max_restarts: 3` budget on the 4th kill and brings down the whole application; the first three kills each restart cleanly, verified directly (round-5 reviewer: 3 kills of `DatabaseWriter` survive, the 4th brings the app down). `Consigliere.Registry` is a strictly worse, separate case, not a smaller instance of the same rule: killing its own supervisor pid brings the whole application down on a **single** kill, because its `PIDPartition0` child does die (it is not an orphan that persists) but slowly, so that one external kill's restart attempt collides with the still-dying pid three times internally, consuming the entire budget from one kill rather than four. Both are understood, reproduced independently multiple times, and accepted as intentional fail-closed outcomes under this project's daemon-restart-is-routine design; not something Spike B should patch around.
- This spike's `RunnerProcess` spawns a fake harness via a plain Erlang Port, not the real external Go runner from `docs/protocols/runner.md`. Spike C (daemon-bound termination) is where a real process-group-based, verified-kill runner belongs; this spike deliberately scoped the harness to the minimum needed to prove coordinator/runner independence.
